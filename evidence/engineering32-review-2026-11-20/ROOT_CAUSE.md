# Engineering32 root-cause account

The verified admission defect was a sticky result from a single `ifAvailable` request: a transient holder produced `occupied`, and holder termination did not schedule another request. This is supported by the exact original state-machine implementation, the independent Brain probe reported in the authorization, and `runtime-handoff-unit-red2`. The ordinary baseline browser experiment passed its three viewports. It did not reproduce or explain every one of Engineering31's 29 supervised Retry actions. Those original runs remain externally assisted evidence, not unassisted success.

The original browser helper creates an app target and then navigates it again. It can exercise document handoff, but that fact alone does not diagnose a particular historical failure. The original runs lack document/request/callback traces sufficient to assign every intervention. A later `navigator.locks.query()` snapshot with zero held locks cannot establish permission or reconstruct callback timing.

The replacement uses one queued exclusive request, with a three-second abort bound. Only its current actual lock callback admits private startup. Pending requests coalesce; timeout, departure and successor identity retire callbacks. A callback from an aborted/superseded attempt returns without retaining a lock or publishing. Unsupported/rejected coordination refuses. There is no polling or automatic retry loop, lock stealing, early release on blur/route/unmount, or query-based admission. A sustained holder ends in explicit refusal/retry. [Web Locks API](https://www.w3.org/TR/web-locks/) defines the queue/callback/holding model used here.

The finite runtime-only matrix was declared before execution: three fresh browser profiles at each of 375×667, 390×844 and 430×932, including startup, same-tab reload, direct entry and history return. Separate induced tests cover a closing holder, sustained real second tab, explicit recovery after termination, pending-document reload, unsupported and rejected coordination. All earlier eligible series and failures remain in the execution receipts; the final-source result is summarized in README.

The durable-command investigation found a second concrete defect in the first implementation. `connected-working1` verified encrypted preparation/readback, force-terminated the owned browser tree, reopened the same synthetic profile and could not find the newest original command; older decisions remained. A memory readback from browser localStorage was insufficient at that crash boundary. The strict IndexedDB recovery row then survived with identical before/after ciphertext hashes in `connected-working2`, which separately exposed focus moving to body when an async inspection disabled its button. The focus correction preserved the original assertion.

`connected-working3` passed the complete 375px journey, but at 390px the prepared recovery ciphertext survived while the completion ledger ciphertext differed after the forced browser restart. This exposed the same durability dependency in the predecessor ledger. The first repair moved the encrypted routine ledger and recovery collection to strict browser transactions. An explicit mutation/preparation preserves an authenticated legacy predecessor with its evidence; a read never migrates. Ambiguous legacy-only shadows after a durable store exists refuse rather than resurrect. Existing native raw storage, encryption, content-key custody and domain predecessor/source fences remain in place.

Acknowledgement marks and retains original command evidence for inspection instead of deleting its only copy. Acknowledged commands cannot replay. All retained decisions count toward the explicit 100-record/512,000-byte limit; capacity refuses new preparation without eviction. Purpose/full cleanup owns both browser locations. This is a deliberate bounded retention tradeoff, not unlimited history or hosted custody.

The [IndexedDB specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/IndexedDB/#transaction-durability-hint) describes strict transaction durability and completion. This is a browser storage contract plus local forced-process evidence. It does not qualify hardware power loss, physical iPhone storage, provider/backend custody or scientific claims.

`final-connected3` then exposed a distinct privacy dependency: after actual owner withdrawal and immediate forced process termination, the localStorage lifecycle/consent receipts rolled back and the app reopened its earlier active shell. The durable routine rows remained erased. The repair adds the original encrypted lifecycle and consent receipts to the same fixed strict-transaction adapter, under unchanged policy and cryptography. A successor process audits raw health-purpose presence before reporting a completed withdrawal; retained or unreadable bytes reopen existing guarded cleanup while processing remains closed. This is not permission inferred from a database flag.

`final-connected4` proved withdrawal/restart at 375px, then the next viewport's onboarding timed out. Its earlier fixture reset only knew localStorage, while the new durable authority correctly retained withdrawal. The committed driver now creates a genuinely fresh browser profile for every viewport, retains that same profile for its within-journey forced restarts, and never clears private storage to make recovery pass. A legacy read compatibility review also corrected the DB-absent lifecycle/consent fallback; the isolated raw-port fixture proves original opaque bytes remain readable without a database open.

Driver-only failures also include an incomplete accessible Row name (`final-connected1`) and a text helper inserting spaces between JSX text fragments (`final-connected2`). The final driver checks the complete rendered withdrawal and cleanup status. No product timeout was enlarged and no substantive crash, ciphertext, authority or focus assertion was removed.
