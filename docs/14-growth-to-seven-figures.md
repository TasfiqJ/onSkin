This is a real but minority-odds bet tilted by execution, not a base-case outcome. *(Confidence: high that the base rate is brutal; the upside is real but conditional.)*

## Key Findings

1. **The unit economics forbid paid UA and mandate organic.** Max CAC ~$10.67/payer (3:1 on $32 Y1 LTV) implies a ~$0.32 CPI ceiling against a $3 to $6 H&F CPI floor. Organic is the only channel that closes the model. *(Confidence: high.)*

2. **The brand constraint and the growth strategy are the same thing.** The hype tactics OnSkin refuses (fear, before/afters, "X% accurate") are exactly the tactics that only monetize through paid ad creative OnSkin cannot afford. Trust-first is not a handicap; it is the forced-and-correct strategy. *(Confidence: high.)*

3. **The shareable artifact is the engine; the referral program is a secondary amplifier.** Yuka, Spotify Wrapped, and Dropbox all grew on an artifact first; Dropbox added referral only after ~1/3 of signups were already word-of-mouth. Build the Conflict Card first, the referral program second. *(Confidence: high on the sequencing principle.)*

4. **ASO is the near-free, highest-leverage channel and it rewards honesty.** ~65% of iOS installs start with a search; the stores reject the hype OnSkin already avoids; a 4.5-star rating is the most credible non-self-authored proof a skeptical buyer sees. *(Confidence: high.)*

5. **Organic short-form works on volume, not virality, and on education, not hype.** The winning pattern is many claim-safe videos across a small account fleet (the "is your routine conflicting?" format anchored to the 2025 Pediatrics study), not one viral hit. *(Confidence: high on the volume model; medium on whether the claim-safe register reaches enough scale.)*

6. **Credentialed-creator seeding (gift, then affiliate-on-subscription, then whitelist-winners) is the only creator model that fits the brand.** Commission tied to the subscription, never to recommended products, preserves church-and-state. FTC disclosure of gifts is mandatory and binds both sides. *(Confidence: high.)*

7. **The funnel, not the paywall art, makes the money, and the high-converting funnel is fully claim-safe.** A personalized quiz ending in a real conflict finding (the aha moment) placed immediately before the paywall converts 5x to 6x better, with zero hype. Keep the reverse trial; fix its placement and follow-up. *(Confidence: high.)*

8. **The retention engine is the photo timeline plus a no-guilt habit loop, aimed at the 66-day automaticity window.** Skincare's core churn driver is invisible progress; the photo timeline is the on-brand fix. AI-novelty churns ~30% faster, so OnSkin's refusal of an AI score is a retention asset. *(Confidence: high.)*

9. **The wedge is contested and the base rate is brutal, so this is a tilt-the-odds plan, not a guarantee.** HadaBuddy undercuts at $29.99/yr; the median sub app reaches ~$100K ARR; ~<0.01% of apps are "financial successes" (a dated but directional Gartner figure). Seven figures is the upside tail, earned by execution. *(Confidence: high.)*

10. **The critical path is still the launch-gate blockers, not growth tactics.** None of this runs while `shippableRules()` returns `[]` in production (no derm sign-off), the catalog is unseeded, there is no backend, no payments, and no native build. Growth follows launch-readiness, not the reverse. *(Confidence: high.)*

## Details

### 1. The structural truth, and the math worked in full

The entire strategy follows from one calculation. Do it once and the channel choices become forced.

**Step 1, payers needed.** $1,000,000 ARR divided by a ~$45 gross annual price is **~22,200 paying annual subscribers**. Apple and Google take 15% (the small-business program, under $1M/yr) to 30%, so on a net-revenue basis the figure is **~24,000 to 30,000 payers**. (RevenueCat price-tier data; standard store fees.)

**Step 2, installs needed.** Download-to-paid for a high-priced, hard-paywall Health & Fitness app runs ~2.5 to 3% (RevenueCat: 2.66% high-priced tier vs 1.49% low-priced; Adapty: ~3.7% direct H&F install-to-paid, but ~1.78% for an onboarding-paywall-with-trial, so plan conservatively). At 3% you need ~740,000 installs; at 2.5%, ~888,000; at 2%, ~1,110,000. **Plan for ~0.7M to 1.1M lifetime installs.** Critically: the often-quoted "35 to 42% trial-to-paid" rate is conditioned on users who **start** a trial; install-to-trial is only ~9.5% globally (14.5% North America), so always model **install-to-paid (~2 to 3%)**, never trial-to-paid, or you will undercount installs by ~10x.

**Step 3, the CAC ceiling that kills paid.** Year-1 realized LTV per payer is **~$32 (North America)**, ~$25 (Western Europe), ~$23 (global) (RevenueCat). At a 3:1 LTV:CAC guardrail (the industry standard; 4:1 is excellent, 2:1 is marginal, and Calm runs ~3:1 as the benchmark), the **maximum fully-loaded cost-per-payer is ~$10.67**. At a 3% download-to-paid, each payer costs ~33 installs, so the implied **cost-per-install ceiling is ~$0.32**. The actual paid H&F CPI is **$3 to $6** (Business of Apps 2025: iOS $4.70, Android $3.70; even the most efficient eCPI ~$1.19 is ~4x over the ceiling). **Paid acquisition is structurally unprofitable at this price, full stop.**

**The conclusion is not a preference, it is arithmetic:** budget paid UA at ~$0, run the engine on organic word-of-mouth, ASO, content, and earned media, the way Yuka reached $7M+ on $0 marketing. The only paid spend that is justified is a small Apple Search Ads budget used as a measurement instrument (Section 4), not a growth engine.

**In-depth solution, the founder's spreadsheet.** Build one model with these formulas and steer to it weekly:
- `payers_needed = ARR_target / (gross_annual_price * (1 - store_fee))`
- `installs_needed = payers_needed / download_to_paid`
- `max_CAC_per_payer = Y1_LTV_per_payer / 3`
- `CPI_ceiling = max_CAC_per_payer * download_to_paid`
- Track the live funnel: installs to paywall-views to trial-starts to Day-1-retained to paid to renewed (PostHog), joined to realized LTV and churn (RevenueCat). The dashboard tells you whether you are install-constrained or conversion-constrained, which need opposite fixes.

### 2. Is this truly the path? The validation, with the kill-case unsoftened

**What is validated by the data and by real companies:**
- The organic-only path is the **only** profitable path at $45/yr (Section 1), and Yuka proves it reaches seven figures in OnSkin's exact category with OnSkin's exact refusals (no ads, no brand money, no affiliate-in-recommendations).
- Honest, educational content wins skincare: **CeraVe** grew ~15x to 16x ($140M in 2017 to $2.2B in 2024) on dermatologist-led education, no before/afters, no fear. (Dollar figures are third-party estimates; the strategy is well-documented.)
- The calm, non-manipulative retention model retains, not loses: **Finch** reached ~$30M+ ARR, bootstrapped, with a 4.95 rating on an explicitly no-guilt design, to a 75%-women, 25-to-35 audience that overlaps OnSkin's.

**The kill-case, stated honestly (this is the bear case the research's adversarial angle did not soft-pedal):**
1. **The base rate is brutal.** The median subscription app reaches ~$8,300 MRR (~$100K ARR) by 18 months; the top-versus-bottom gap is widening (top performers now ~400x the bottom quartile). A solo, pre-launch app hitting $1M ARR is a **top-quartile-execution, multi-year upside case.** Honest probability: low-single-digit to low-teens percent.
2. **You cede the cheap-viral half of short-form.** Verified: sensational skincare content (pimple-popping, 804M views) out-reaches education (324M) on the algorithm (Australasian Journal of Dermatology, 2025). OnSkin cannot do fear or before/afters, so it must win the slower credibility game, which takes longer and needs real derm creators (not zero effort).
3. **Year-1 churn worsened to ~72%, with 35% of annual cancels in month 1.** Acquisition is half the battle; without month-1 retention you fill a leaking bucket.
4. **The wedge is contested and undercut.** HadaBuddy ships shelf-scan plus 150+ conflict rules plus an AI advisor at **$29.99/yr** (verified live), and a Google Play app is literally named "OnSkin - Skincare Scanner" (a real trademark and ASO risk).
5. **Yuka is only partially replicable.** It had a red/green score (the screenshot gimmick OnSkin deliberately removed), a food-first daily wedge, a pre-seeded community, and roughly five years. Calling OnSkin a Yuka clone overstates transferability; OnSkin must engineer its own equally-shareable, claim-safe hook (the Conflict Card) to substitute.

**Net validation.** Yes, this is the right strategy, because the unit economics force it and Yuka plus CeraVe prove the honest version reaches seven figures. It is not a sure thing; it is a real but minority-odds bet that execution tilts. The conditions that must hold to win are stated in Section 14.

### 3. The growth engine to build first: the "Shelf Conflict Card"

Across every channel the research converged on one mechanism: **the shareable artifact is the engine.** Yuka's entire $0-marketing growth runs on one viscerally legible object that people physically turn their phone to show a friend. Spotify Wrapped turned private data into ~400M TikTok views in three days and ~10.5M active shares. Dropbox's referral only worked because ~1/3 of signups were already word-of-mouth.

**OnSkin's equivalent is the Shelf Conflict Card.** It is more personal and more shareable than Yuka's score, because it is about products the user already owns.

**Specification (in-depth solution):**
- **Trigger:** after a user adds 2+ products, surface a full-screen card titled "Your shelf, checked for conflicts."
- **Hero moment:** a single, one-glance verdict, a red conflict flag between two owned products ("Retinol + AHA, same night, higher irritation risk"), with the evidence grade the conflict engine already computes.
- **Legibility rule:** readable in under one second (Yuka's mechanic). One glance equals "I need to show someone this." The "Maya" worked loop, below, makes this concrete.
- **Copy discipline:** calm and grade-anchored ("higher irritation risk"), never fear-bait ("this is damaging your skin"). Fear-bait betrays the brand and invites the "is it a scam?" backlash Yuka now fights.
- **Export:** one-tap render to a 1080x1920 Story image with a subtle "Checked with OnSkin" watermark plus a deep link or QR. Build with `react-native-view-shot` (compose the high-res image) plus `expo-sharing` (the OS share sheet); deep links via Branch or Expo Linking so every share is attributable and a viewer taps straight into that exact check.
- **Screenshot-first design:** assume most sharing is raw screenshots, so the on-screen card must look intentional screenshotted, not just the export.
- **Church-and-state:** the card never contains a commissioned product link. The instant a shared card looks like it steers to a paid product, the trust positioning collapses.

**Worked example (the loop made concrete).** Maya adds her Vitamin C serum, Retinol 0.3%, and Glycolic 7% toner. The card flags "Retinol + Glycolic acid, same night, higher irritation risk, evidence grade C," with the calm resolution "alternate nights." Maya is surprised it caught a conflict in her **own** routine, screenshots it, and posts it to her Story with the watermark. Three friends tap the deep link, land on the exact check, and run their own shelves. That is the Yuka in-aisle loop, in skincare, with no ad spend. This single feature is the highest-leverage build on the roadmap.

### 4. Channel One: App Store Optimization (the near-free, highest-leverage channel)

**Why it fits perfectly.** ~65% of iOS installs and ~58% of Play installs start with a search, so as an unknown brand OnSkin is discovered via descriptive terms, not awareness. The stores actively reject the hype OnSkin already refuses, so a claim-safe listing is simultaneously the compliant and the on-brand listing. And the ratings flywheel is a **trust** asset, not a growth hack.

**Step-by-step (how / where / why):**

1. **Build the keyword universe around intent, not brand.** Seed: "ingredient checker", "skincare ingredient checker", "cosmetic/product scanner", "skincare routine", "routine planner", "skin cycling", "is this skincare safe", "check ingredients". Pull difficulty in **AppTweak** or **MobileAction**, then **validate every keyword against live Apple Search Ads "Search Match" impressions** before trusting it. Critical 2026 caveat: in late Sept/Oct 2025 Apple's keyword-popularity data (which every ASO tool resells) collapsed, US keywords with a popularity score above 5 fell from 165,875 to 39,254 in four days (-77.4%), so **all tool "search volume" is now directional only**; live ASA impression data is the ground truth.

2. **Write the iOS metadata to the character limit, claim-safe.** Title (30 chars): "OnSkin: Ingredient Checker". Subtitle (30): a second high-intent phrase ("Scan, check, build routines"). Keyword field (100 chars, hidden, the single highest-ROI 100 characters in the app): comma-separated, **no spaces, no repeats of words in the title/subtitle, singular forms** (Apple auto-pluralizes), for example `cosmetic,product,scanner,ingredients,routine,planner,cycling,safe,skin,beauty,analyzer`. Google Play has no hidden field; weave keywords naturally into the 80-char short description and the long description (Play indexes both). **Never** "treats", "cures", "clears acne", "X% accurate"; both stores reject medical/efficacy overclaims and it breaks the brand.

3. **Design the first 3 screenshots to land the value prop in ~6 words each**, because only ~17% of visitors scroll past screenshot 1 (the first screenshot was the winning A/B variable in ~54% of tests). Screenshot 1: the conflict result plus "Spot ingredient conflicts, evidence-graded." Screenshot 2: the routine builder plus "Build your routine. No guesswork." Screenshot 3: the privacy promise plus "Your photos stay on your phone." Real UI, calm premium palette, no fake before/afters. Apple now indexes caption text, so put a keyword in at least one caption. Add a short muted autoplay app-preview of one scan end to end.

4. **Treat the icon as a first-class conversion asset and A/B test it.** It carries the largest single creative uplift (+22.8% App Store, +20.2% Play). Test 2 to 3 candidates free via **Google Play Store Listing Experiments** (live traffic), and SplitMetrics for iOS pre-launch. Pick the measured winner, not the founder's favorite.

5. **Wire a satisfaction-timed in-app review prompt** to start the ratings flywheel, OnSkin's most trust-aligned conversion multiplier (3.6 to 4.2 stars is ~+60% conversion; ~90% of featured apps are 4.0+). Call `SKStoreReviewController.requestReview()` (iOS) or the Play In-App Review API **after a clear win** (first real conflict caught, first completed skin-cycling week), never on launch or mid-task, respecting Apple's cap of 3 prompts per user per year. Reply to every public review, especially critical ones; it lifts both rating and ranking.

6. **Run a small Apple Search Ads budget as the ASO complement and the keyword lab.** Three campaign types: brand defense on "OnSkin" (a same-named competitor exists and can park an ad above you), Search Match discovery (let Apple surface converting terms, then graduate winners into the organic keyword field), and generic high-intent ("ingredient checker"). Known economics: H&F CPI ~$3.83, CVR ~46%; ASA produces a ~20 to 30% organic-install halo. Build Custom Product Pages per intent (a privacy-led page for privacy searchers, an ingredient-safety page for conflict searchers; CPPs add ~+8.6 percentage points of conversion and now surface in organic search).

7. **Localize only the first 2 to 3 screenshots plus metadata into 1 to 2 high-value non-English markets, after English converts.** Screenshot-text localization alone lifts conversion +33 to 36%, and adding a language correlated with ~128% more downloads per country, far cheaper than full app localization. German and French are large beauty markets with privacy/clean-beauty cultures that fit OnSkin. Use a native speaker for the few load-bearing caption words.

8. **Iterate on a fixed cadence.** Track keyword rankings, page conversion, install volume, and rating weekly; refresh screenshots/metadata 2 to 4 times a year; A/B test before shipping any change; re-pull keywords each quarter and re-validate against live ASA data.

**Tools:** AppTweak (main console), MobileAction (ASA + keyword intelligence), App Radar (affordable all-in-one with review management), SplitMetrics plus Google Play Experiments (creative A/B), AppFollow (review monitoring/replies), Apple Search Ads Advanced (the keyword lab).

**Critical pitfalls:** clear the "OnSkin" name with a trademark check before launch (the Play app is a real conflict); never use hype/medical claims in metadata; fire the review prompt only at a satisfaction moment; do not bury the value prop below screenshot 1; do not trust tool "volume" post-Oct-2025.

### 5. Channel Two: Organic short-form (the content volume game)

**Why it fits.** Authentic short-form is near-free at OnSkin's scale, skincare is TikTok's highest-engagement vertical, and polished "ad-looking" beauty content spikes CPM ~40%, so OnSkin's calm, real-UI style is an advantage. Gifted/authentic content gets ~2.19% engagement vs ~1.94% for paid.

**Step-by-step:**

1. **Set up the account fleet before paying a single creator.** One founder/face account ("the person who built the no-hype skincare app"), one OnSkin brand account, 1 to 2 faceless "Skin Notes / OnSkin Lab" slideshow accounts. Cross-post every cut to Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts (same vertical video, ~3x reach for the same production cost). Edit in **CapCut + Canva**, batch-shoot a week at a time.

2. **Commit to volume, not virality.** Cal AI ran 12+ accounts and 1,000+ videos; no single video reliably hits. Target **1 to 3 posts/day across the fleet (~30 to 100/month) for the first 90 days.**

3. **Lock the 4 claim-safe formats and a 7-second silent-hook discipline:**
   - **Conflict demo:** open the app, add 2 to 3 products, show the engine flag a real interaction ("is your routine fighting itself?"), framed as a check, never a cure.
   - **Talking-head myth-bust:** founder or a derm answers one viral myth ("do you really need 6 steps?"), citing the 2025 Pediatrics study (Northwestern/AAP: teen TikTok routines averaged 6 products, 11+ active ingredients up to 21, only 26% included sunscreen, ~$168/month). This is the perfect claim-safe, fear-free hook: science, not scare tactics.
   - **Faceless slideshow:** text-overlay "green-flag / red-flag routine", "3 actives that should not share a shelf".
   - **Repost winners** to Reels/Shorts.
   - **Rule:** the idea must read in a 7-second silent clip with text overlay; lead with curiosity/stakes ("your $168 routine might be why your barrier is wrecked"), never a fear scream or "cures acne."

4. **Instrument installs-per-view, not vanity views.** Cal AI's early benchmark was ~10 installs per 1,000 views; a 56M-view video that does not convert is worthless. Use per-creator/vanity links and an MMP (Section 7) plus PostHog for the post-install funnel.

**In-depth solution, the format brief.** Maintain a one-page internal brief that bans before/afters, "cures/treats/clears", "X% accurate", and fear-mongering, and lists the approved hooks. Script the **demonstration** (run your shelf through it), never the **verdict**.

**Pitfalls:** do not copy Cal AI's "accuracy test" register (you refused a score); keep the Pediatrics study framed as "what the science says about over-layering", not "your routine is destroying your skin"; do not let the fleet look like an ad farm (each account needs a distinct authentic voice); do not target minors given the teen-skincare discourse (lead with adult skin-barrier education); the founder face matters (a faceless-only presence underperforms the "solo founder who refuses to lie to you" story).

### 6. Channel Three: Credentialed-creator seeding ("DermTok"), not influencer ads

**Why it fits.** This is how CeraVe became #1 skincare on TikTok (50+ dermatologist creators, education not hype), and it is the only creator model that does not betray the brand. Run it as a three-stage funnel.

1. **Write the claim-safe brief before contacting anyone.** It (a) bans "treats/cures/clears", before/afters, "X% accurate"; (b) mandates the FTC disclosure language and placement (step 5); (c) gives approved demonstration hooks ("I ran my shelf through OnSkin and it flagged my retinol + AHA"). Provide a free annual subscription and let the creator generate their own real conflict report on camera. **Never script claims; script the demonstration.**

2. **Build a 30 to 60 person roster weighted to credentialed + nano/micro educators.** ~5 to 10 "trust anchors" (board-certified derms, estheticians, cosmetic chemists who already post evidence-based, hype-averse content); ~20 to 40 nano/micro science-skincare creators (1K to 50K followers, ~7% engagement vs ~1.7% for macro). **Vet every handle** for fake followers (Modash, GRIN, HypeAuditor) before gifting; audits routinely find only 40 to 60% of likes are real on bad accounts.

3. **Stage 1, gift first, ask for nothing.** Comp a free annual subscription via RevenueCat promo codes plus a short no-strings note ("run your own shelf, no obligation"). Tell them that if they post, FTC rules require disclosing the free gift. goPure got 147 videos and >$200K influenced revenue at $0 media cost from seeding (vendor-reported, treat the headline ROI as directional).

4. **Stage 2, convert genuine advocates to ShopMy affiliate, on the subscription only.** A hybrid deal: a modest flat fee (or $0 for nano) plus a commission on each annual subscription they drive (15 to 25% of the ~$45, or a flat $10 to 15 per new subscriber for the first term). **The commission is tied to subscriptions, never to any product the app recommends**, preserving church-and-state, and say so publicly. Use **ShopMy** as the primary rail (beauty-native, 1,000+ follower floor, weekly payouts, ~2.9% transaction fee, no required dashboard to start, now valued ~$1.5B); LTK as secondary. ELEMIS got 30% YoY revenue and 1.4x ROAS from just 15 affiliate creators on LTK.

5. **Stage 3, whitelist only proven winners** as TikTok Partnership Ads (formerly Spark Ads: +142% engagement, +43% conversion vs non-Spark), running from the creator's own handle, re-screened against the brief. Start tiny ($50 to 150/day per video) and scale only what beats your CAC target.

6. **Enforce FTC disclosure on every post.** The 2023 Endorsement Guides bind **gifts** like cash and hold **both** brand and creator liable. Require, in writing, that disclosure is clear and conspicuous, spoken on camera and on-screen ("Free annual subscription from OnSkin #ad"), in the video itself (not just the caption, not buried in hashtags, not the platform toggle alone). For a trust brand, a sloppy disclosure is existential.

**In-depth solution, the CAC discipline.** Track per-creator and per-code: installs, trial starts, paid annual conversions, refund/churn (RevenueCat promo codes joined to PostHog plus ShopMy UTMs). Compute creator CAC = (gifting + fees + commissions + paid amplification) / paid subscribers; hold it well under the ~$10.67 ceiling; give each creator a renewal score and re-book only the top tier. Stage-3 whitelisting should fund itself from proven CAC.

**Pitfalls:** do not buy macro/hype posts (worse conversion, contradicts the brand); never let commission touch recommendations; gifts are not exempt from disclosure; do not accept hashtag-buried #ad or the platform toggle alone; do not plan around TikTok Creator Marketplace (sunset 2025, now invite-gated TikTok One); brief credentialed derms just as strictly (a derm implying OnSkin "treats" or "cures" is still a banned claim).

### 7. Channel Four: the word-of-mouth artifact loop and referral

Built on Section 3's Conflict Card. The mechanism, not a referral program, is the engine.

1. **Ship the Conflict Card plus one-tap watermarked Story export plus deep link** (Section 3). This is the centerpiece.

2. **Build a quarterly "Skin Notes Recap"**, OnSkin's Spotify Wrapped: routine-consistency streak, conflicts avoided, skin-cycling adherence, products added, in calm factual language, no photos (they stay on-device), each tile opt-in to share. Wrapped drove +40% engagement and ~2.1M mentions in 48 hours; a recap gives OnSkin a calendarable word-of-mouth spike each quarter and frames the brand around discipline, not hype.

3. **Engineer the in-aisle loop.** Make the conflict check fast enough to run on a friend's shelf in a store or bathroom; add a "check a friend's shelf" mode and gentle prompts at natural moments ("know someone mixing retinol and acids? send them this"). Yuka's founder quantifies that happy users tell 10 to 20 people; make that demo effortless.

4. **Add a non-cash, double-sided referral program only after organic word-of-mouth is visible.** Both sides get bonus subscription time or 20% off the annual plan (fulfilled via RevenueCat, no cash flow), one screen, one link, one reward (simple programs convert 2.6x better; double-sided lifts participation up to 91% and subscription brands see 2.4x). **Never cash** (PayPal's cash-referral was a money-furnace only viable because eBay acquired it) and **never** product discounts that touch recommendations. Dropbox proves the sequencing: it grew 100K to 4M in 15 months on a double-sided reward, but ~1/3 of signups were already word-of-mouth first.

5. **Instrument K-factor** (K = invites-per-user x conversion-per-invite) per cohort in PostHog. Plan for realistic consumer K of 0.15 to 0.7 (K above 1 is rare and temporary); only ~2 to 5% of invitations are accepted, so the real leverage is the card's organic screenshot-ability, not the invite button. Model word-of-mouth as a CAC-reducer on top of organic, not the sole engine.

6. **Activate earned media without paying for hype.** Pitch the refusals as the story (no AI score, no fake before/afters, photos on-device, commission out of recommendations) to skincare-skeptic dermatologists and ingredient-honest creators; give them the Conflict Card as a ready-made screenshottable proof object. Publish a calm, high-quality newsletter (Yuka's newsletters hit 500K to 1M views) that creators cite. Yuka's watershed was one consumer TikTok, then word-of-mouth took over.

**Pitfalls:** do not lead with cash or a referral program; do not let the card drift into fear-bait; never couple the artifact to a commissioned product; photos must stay on-device (build recap/share artifacts from non-photo data); do not model the business on K above 1; sharing must be unbundled and consent-respecting per docs/01.

### 8. The conversion funnel: quiz to aha to paywall to trial

**Why it fits.** The highest-converting 2026 funnels (Noom, Flo, Zoe, Cal AI) are built on the exact mechanism OnSkin can use honestly: a personalized quiz culminating in a real, specific result the user invested effort to get. OnSkin's aha is an actual ingredient-conflict finding plus a generated routine, both factual and personal, requiring zero "treats/cures/X% accurate" language.

1. **Build a 6 to 10 step personalized skin quiz as the onboarding spine.** Ask, with sliders and single-tap chips for micro-commitments: primary goal (first screen), skin type, top concerns, current products/actives, sun exposure, sensitivity, age band, pregnancy/retinoid flag. End by generating the AM/PM routine skeleton **and** surfacing one genuine conflict-engine finding ("you listed retinol + an AHA, here is the conflict and the safe schedule"). That finding is the aha. Reference the user's own inputs in the result and the paywall headline ("We found 2 conflicts in your current routine"). Quiz funnels lift conversion up to 60% (one case moved download-to-payer 7% to 13%) because effort plus a personalized result create commitment before price.

2. **Place the paywall immediately after the result screen, at the motivation peak.** Sequence: quiz, a brief "building your routine" crafted delay, result plus conflict finding, paywall on the very next screen. Do not add screens between the result and the paywall. Upfront/aha paywalls convert 5x to 6x higher (~12% vs ~2%); Greg went 3% to 15% trial signup, Rootd ~5x revenue, by moving the paywall earlier. ~82 to 90% of trial starts happen on Day 0, so the first session is the conversion window.

3. **Keep the reverse trial as the trust-aligned default, framed against the annual plan.** Primary CTA: start the reverse trial (full access, no card, honest "we will remind you before it ends"). Default-select annual ($45 to 50/yr) shown as ~$0.12/day next to the monthly anchor, with a calm Free-vs-Premium comparison table. No countdown timers, no fake scarcity. H&F is annual-dominant (60 to 68% of revenue) and leads trial-to-paid at 35.0%; plan-duration tests show a 58.7% LTV win rate, and H&F annual plans earn ~4.5x the LTV of cheap ones.

4. **Choose a 7-day trial, not 3-day.** A skincare routine needs at least one cycle to feel valuable. Sub-4-day trials convert at 25.5% with 55.4% Day-0 cancellation; 7-day trials drop Day-0 cancellation to 39.8% and convert ~37%; H&F users convert either Day 0 or Days 4 to 7, so a 3-day window cuts off the second peak. Send a value reminder (not a sales push) on Day 5 to 6.

5. **Add a single post-dismissal welcome offer** for users who decline (a value reveal, the full conflict report for their inputs, or a transparent one-time founding-member rate), yielding ~10 to 15% ARPU lift without devaluing the brand. Show it once; never nag.

6. **Run a real paywall A/B loop from day one, testing the funnel, not the art.** Cal AI ran 123 experiments (61 on the onboarding paywall) and grew revenue 3x in 10 months with +31% trial-to-paid. Use **Superwall** (no-code remote paywall A/B, the tool Cal AI used) layered on **RevenueCat** (the subscription/entitlement backbone, already in OnSkin's stack), with **PostHog** for quiz-step drop-off. Test quiz length, which conflict finding is the aha, annual-default framing, trial copy, and the post-dismissal offer, one variable at a time. Price/plan-duration tests lift LTV ~45% of the time even when they do not lift conversion. Add abandoned-checkout recovery once payments are live (~37% of checkout-starters do not finish; abandon paywalls drove 17% of revenue in an 18-app study and cut refunds 6.8% to 3.3%).

**Tools:** RevenueCat (backbone), Superwall (paywall A/B) or Adapty (the all-in-one alternative, RevenueCat-compatible, free to $10K MTR then ~1% of revenue), PostHog (funnel). Skip Purchasely (enterprise, ~$1,800/mo+).

**Pitfalls:** do not import urgency gimmicks (countdown timers, spin wheels, fake scarcity), they violate the brand; do not rip out the reverse trial for a hard paywall (the Day-35 edge converges by Year 1); do not default to 3-day trials; the aha must be a factual finding, never a score or "X% accurate"; do not over-discount; do not add screens between result and paywall; do not require photos in the quiz (personalize from typed inputs only, per the local-photos rule).

### 9. The retention engine: a calm habit loop plus the photo timeline

**Why it is the make-or-break.** Skincare's value is invisible day to day, which is why users quit before the routine works. Year-1 churn is ~72%, 35% of annual cancels hit month 1, and AI-novelty apps churn ~30% faster (21.1% vs 30.7% annual retention), so OnSkin's refusal of an AI score is a retention **asset**.

1. **Make the photo-progress timeline the retention spine, and force the first baseline photo into session one.** After the user builds a routine, prompt one optional baseline photo: "Take one photo now. In 6 weeks you will see what daily observation cannot." Store on-device only (`expo-camera`, `expo-image-manipulator`, `expo-file-system`/`expo-secure-store`; no upload, per docs/01). Auto-generate a side-by-side at Day 14, 28, and 42, surfaced as a quiet "Your 6-week view is ready" card, never a hype before/after. This is the most defensible, claim-safe reward OnSkin can ship: the user's own evidence.

2. **Ship a calm AM/PM check-off loop with two fixed daily cues**, and make session one deliver a felt aha before any paywall friction. A morning "Good morning, your AM routine" card and an evening "Wind down, your PM routine" card, each a one-tap check-off; on first run immediately show the conflict-engine result for their actual products so value lands in under 60 seconds (mirroring Oura's single-clear-daily-win pattern). 84% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen Day 0 to 1, so front-load value.

3. **Send few, personalized, perfectly-timed pushes, never frequency spam.** Default to exactly two opt-in pushes/day (the AM and PM cues), each at the user's stated routine time, deep-linked to that routine's check-off, with copy personalized to their routine ("time for your PM vitamin C step"), never guilt ("do not break your streak"). Opted-in users who get any push in the first 90 days retain ~3x better (the Airship benchmark is a 2016 cohort, so treat the multiplier as directional); behavior/timing targeting adds up to 3x and ~+30% interaction. Add a one-tap "fewer reminders" control. Use `expo-notifications` for local scheduling first (no backend cost, privacy-friendly).

4. **Adopt "progress, not punishment" streaks.** Track a soft, accumulating consistency path (a filling path/garden, like Gentler Streak's Activity Path or Finch's growing pet) where a missed day pauses but never zeroes or shames. No red "streak lost" states, no fear pushes, no countdown-to-death. Celebrate milestones positively at 7, 14, 30, 66 days, and frame skin-cycling rest nights as success. Finch ($30M+ ARR, 4.95 rating, no VC, no aggressive push) and Gentler Streak prove a no-guilt loop retains a women-25-to-35 wellness audience as well as or better than streak-shaming; Duolingo's loss-aversion guilt is exactly the manipulative mechanic OnSkin must not import. Anchor to the 66-day automaticity window (Lally 2010, mean 66 days, range 18 to 254, cue consistency over perfection).

5. **Instrument the honest North Star: check-off consistency toward 66 days, not DAU.** In PostHog define an "activated" user (completed first AM and PM check-off plus a baseline photo in week 1), track the share reaching 7/14/30/66 check-off days, and join to annual renewal in RevenueCat. North Star: "share of subscribers who reach 66 lifetime check-offs in their first annual term." Annual H&F renewal is only ~25%, so the in-app habit, not pricing, decides Year-1 renewal. Measuring the real habit is the non-engagement-farming KPI; a DAU target is what tempts teams into manipulation.

6. **Recover involuntary churn** via RevenueCat billing retry/grace (involuntary churn is ~31% on Play, ~14% on App Store, pure recoverable revenue), and frame the renewal reminder as value, not a dark pattern.

**Pitfalls:** do not import guilt mechanics; do not "add AI" to chase the 41% LTV bump (the churn tax erases it); do not spam; never fake or imply results in the timeline; do not treat 66 days as a hard reset on a miss; Day 0 to 1 is the whole game.

### 10. Paid amplification, only on proven winners, and only as a measurement instrument

Paid acquisition is structurally unprofitable at $45/yr (Section 1), so it is **not** a growth engine. Two narrow, justified uses:
1. **Apple Search Ads as a keyword lab and brand defense** (Section 4, step 6): a few hundred dollars to surface real keyword demand post-Oct-2025 and to stop a same-named competitor parking above you. The ~20 to 30% organic halo is a bonus, not the rationale.
2. **Spark/Partnership Ads to amplify already-proven organic videos** (Sections 5 and 6): only behind videos that organically demonstrated they convert installs, running from the creator's handle, started tiny and scaled only past the CAC target. This de-risks spend: you pay to scale winners, not to gamble on cold installs at a $3.70 to $4.70 blind CPI.

Anything beyond these (cold app-install campaigns on TikTok/Meta) loses money on every install and burns the limited founder budget. The discipline is organic-first, paid-amplify-winners, never the reverse. Measurement requires an MMP (AppsFlyer, Adjust, or Singular) once paid is live, plus per-creator codes; note the iOS ATT/SKAdNetwork/AdAttributionKit reality makes blind paid scaling expensive for a small advertiser.

### 11. The launch sequence

1. **Clear the launch-gate blockers first** (the real critical path): B-DERM-REVIEW (so `shippableRules()` stops returning `[]`), B-SUPABASE, the catalog seed, payments, and the native build. No growth runs before this.

4. **Then the organic engine** (Sections 4 to 9 in parallel), then paid amplification of winners (Section 10).

### 12. The metrics dashboard, guardrails, and the worked funnel

**Guardrail targets to hold:** download-to-paid ~2.5 to 3%; trial-to-paid ~40%; annual mix >90%; LTV:CAC >= 3:1 (fully-loaded CAC/payer <= ~$10.67); month-1 retention defended; check-off-consistency-to-66-days as the retention North Star.

**The worked funnel (one scenario, to make the scale concrete).** Suppose OnSkin reaches 800,000 lifetime installs over ~2 to 3 years of organic effort. At a 14.5% North-America install-to-trial and a 40% trial-to-paid, that is ~46,400 payers; net of a 15% store fee at $45, ~$1.77M gross subscription revenue, of which ARR depends on renewal. At the harsher ~2.5% blended install-to-paid, 800,000 installs is ~20,000 payers (~$765K net first-year), so renewal and a second cohort year carry it past $1M ARR. The lesson: **~0.7M to 1.1M installs and a defended ~40% trial-to-paid and >25% annual renewal are the three dials**, and all three are organic/retention problems, not paid-spend problems.

**Instrument it:** PostHog for the install-to-paywall-to-trial-to-Day1-to-paid-to-renew funnel and the retention cohorts; RevenueCat for realized LTV, churn, and renewal truth; an MMP plus per-creator codes for channel attribution once paid amplification starts. Re-measure against OnSkin's own cohort within 90 days, because the 2.5 to 3% and $32 LTV are category medians, and a pre-launch app with a thin catalog may underperform them at first, then re-run the Section 1 math.

### 14. In-depth solutions to the failure modes (the kill-case answered)

- **"CAC > LTV will kill it."** Solution: budget paid UA at ~$0 by construction; use paid only to amplify proven organic winners (Section 10). The model is designed so the dominant channels (ASO, content, word-of-mouth) have near-zero marginal cost, exactly Yuka's structure.
- **"The trust brand cannot win hype-driven short-form."** Solution: do not compete for the cheap-viral half; win the credibility half (CeraVe's path) via the 2025 Pediatrics-study education hook, credentialed-creator seeding, and the genuinely-surprising Conflict Card. Accept it is slower and instrument it patiently.
- **"Year-1 churn (72%) eats the funnel."** Solution: the Section 9 retention engine (photo timeline plus calm habit loop plus involuntary-churn recovery), with check-off-consistency-to-66-days as the North Star, raises realized LTV above $32, which raises the CAC ceiling and the odds of clearing 3:1.
- **"HadaBuddy undercuts at $29.99 and the wedge is contested."** Solution: do not compete on price (price down is LTV down, and Section 1 shows you need the high tier); compete on the structural moat the verification confirmed (deterministic conflict correctness, claim-safety, provable independence, privacy, owned longitudinal context) and on the shareable artifact. Clear the trademark before launch.
- **"Yuka is not replicable."** Solution: do not clone Yuka's score (you refused it); build OnSkin's own equally-shareable, claim-safe artifact (the Conflict Card about products the user owns), and replicate the **mechanism** (one-glance shareable truth object plus word-of-mouth), not the gimmick.
- **"The base rate is brutal."** This one is not fully solvable; it is the honest tail nature of the bet. The solution is execution discipline on the few dials that matter (installs, trial-to-paid, annual renewal) and a willingness to run a multi-year, organic, patient game, which is the only game the economics allow.

## Seven-Figure Validation (the honest assessment)

The founder's question, applied to growth rather than a feature: is this the path to a seven-figure business, and is it the best one? The evidence-weighted answer: **yes, it is the right and the only economically-viable path, and Yuka proves the ceiling is real, but it is a top-quartile-execution, multi-year, minority-odds bet, not a base case.**

- **It is the only economically-viable path.** At $45/yr, paid acquisition is ~10x to 19x underwater, so organic word-of-mouth, ASO, content, and earned media are not a preference but the forced strategy. The brand constraint and the strategy are identical. *(Confidence: high.)*
- **The ceiling is proven in-category.** Yuka reached $7.3M revenue, 98.1% subscriptions, on $0 marketing, with OnSkin's exact refusals. CeraVe proves honest education wins skincare. Finch proves calm retention works. *(Confidence: high on the existence proofs; medium on transferability to OnSkin specifically.)*
- **The engine is buildable now.** The Conflict Card, the ASO listing, the quiz-to-paywall funnel, and the calm habit loop are all things OnSkin can ship; the conflict engine, the design tokens, the reverse trial, RevenueCat, and PostHog already exist. *(Confidence: high.)*
- **The risk is real and concentrated in three places:** the base rate (~<0.01% of apps are financial successes; median ~$100K ARR), the structural cede of cheap-viral reach, and Year-1 churn. Each has a mitigation (Section 14), none is fully solvable. *(Confidence: high that these are the binding risks.)*

**Verdict.** Growth, not more product, is the gap to seven figures, and the organic, trust-first, artifact-led plan in this document is the correct response, because the math forces it and the proof exists. It will not be fast and it is not likely in the statistical base-rate sense; it is a patient, multi-year, execution-heavy bet that the rare honest brand can compound word-of-mouth the way Yuka did. The single highest-leverage action is to build the Shelf Conflict Card and clear the launch blockers; everything else amplifies those.

## Synthesis

**(a) The structural truth:** at $45/yr, paid UA is mathematically impossible (~$0.32 CPI ceiling vs $3 to $6 floor), so the entire engine must be organic, which is also the only on-brand option.

**(b) The proof:** Yuka reached $7.3M on $0 marketing with OnSkin's exact refusals; CeraVe proves honest education wins skincare; Finch proves calm retention works. Cal AI is the anti-model (paid plus affiliates), useful only for its content formats and paywall discipline.

**(c) The engine:** the shareable artifact (the Shelf Conflict Card) is the growth engine; build it first; the referral program is a secondary amplifier added only after word-of-mouth is visible.

**(d) The channels:** ASO (near-free, highest-leverage, rewards honesty), organic short-form (volume not virality, education not hype), credentialed-creator seeding (gift then affiliate-on-subscription then whitelist-winners), and the word-of-mouth artifact loop. All four run in parallel.

**(e) The funnel:** a personalized quiz ending in a real conflict finding, placed immediately before the paywall, with the reverse trial kept and a 7-day length, converts 5x to 6x better with zero hype.

**(f) The retention:** the photo timeline plus a no-guilt habit loop aimed at the 66-day automaticity window, with check-off-consistency as the North Star, not DAU; OnSkin's refusal of an AI score is a retention asset.

**(g) Paid:** not a growth engine; only Apple Search Ads as a keyword lab and Spark Ads to amplify proven organic winners.

**(h) The odds:** seven figures is the upside tail (low-single-digit to low-teens percent), earned by patient multi-year execution on three dials (installs, trial-to-paid, annual renewal), all of which are organic/retention problems, not spend problems.

## Caveats (confidence flags)

They are directional, not gospel; do not quote them to investors as hard facts. *Low-to-medium confidence on those specific numbers; high on the underlying mechanisms.*
- **ASO tool "search volume" is structurally unreliable post-Oct-2025** (Apple's popularity data collapsed 77.4%); validate keywords against live Apple Search Ads data, not tool volume. *High confidence on the caveat.*
- **The honest probability of reaching $1M ARR is low** (the base rate: median ~$100K ARR, a dated but directional Gartner <0.01% success figure). This is a top-quartile-execution, multi-year upside case, tilted by execution, not a base-case outcome. *High confidence that the base rate is brutal; the upside is real but conditional.*
- **The whole plan is downstream of launch-readiness.** None of it runs while the conflict engine shows nothing in production (no derm sign-off), the catalog is empty, and there is no backend, payments, or native build. *High confidence.*
