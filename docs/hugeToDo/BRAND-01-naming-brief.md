# BRAND-01 Naming Brief

Date: 2026-07-12 (America/Toronto)
Status: prepared for founder approval; not legal clearance

## Decision to make

Choose a protectable, pronounceable public brand for the iOS skincare product
currently using `Layerwell` as its development identity. The name must work as
an App Store name, spoken recommendation, domain/handle root, subscription
brand, and eventual platform brand. It must not imply medical diagnosis,
treatment, clinical authority, or unreviewed artificial-intelligence output.

## Product in one sentence

A private skincare shelf and routine companion that helps people organize
products they already own, understand reviewed conflicts and sequencing, follow
calm AM/PM plans, track adherence and private progress, and optionally use
community, commerce, trend, and advisor features behind their required safety
and consent gates.

## Positioning

- Primary lane: the private operating system for a person's skincare shelf and
  routine.
- Primary customer promise: use what you own with more clarity and less
  guesswork.
- Emotional tone: calm, capable, kind, evidence-aware, and non-punitive.
- Differentiation: owned-products first; shelf -> insight -> routine -> Today ->
  private progress. It is not primarily a generic barcode scanner, beauty score,
  marketplace, or social feed.
- Expansion: subscriptions, reviewed guidance, progress, creator/community,
  commerce, and professional workflows may sit under the same parent brand.

The underlying product requirements are in
[`PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md`](../PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md), and the full positioning
and competitor context are in [`MASTER_PLAN.md`](../MASTER_PLAN.md).

## Audience

1. Skincare enthusiasts overwhelmed by active ingredients and sequencing.
2. Sensitive-skin beginners seeking calm, private organization rather than
   medical-sounding certainty.
3. Creators and power users who want credible, shareable routine artifacts.
4. A future professional buyer such as an esthetician, without representing the
   consumer app as a clinical service.

## Claims and naming guardrails

The brand must not contain or lean on terms that reasonably suggest:

- diagnosis, treatment, cure, prevention, prescription, doctor, clinic, derm,
  medical, or clinical authority;
- an AI skin score, skin age, guaranteed result, percentage improvement, or
  scientifically proven outcome;
- universal ingredient safety or product compatibility;
- public before/after comparison or cloud photo analysis;
- commission-influenced recommendation quality.

Avoid `Layerwell` and close variations. An incumbent uses the exact name for a
skincare/cosmetic scanner in the same distribution channel; the evidence and
rationale are recorded in [`brand-evidence.md`](../brand-evidence.md).

## Name requirements

| Requirement     | Target                                                                                                           |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Distinctiveness | Prefer suggestive or coined; reject generic or merely descriptive phrases.                                       |
| Category fit    | Should feel credible for shelf/routine/progress without locking the company to scanning.                         |
| Memorability    | One compact word or two short words; visually recognizable at small size.                                        |
| Pronunciation   | One dominant English pronunciation; usable in a podcast or friend referral.                                      |
| Spelling        | A listener should have a strong chance of spelling it after hearing it once.                                     |
| Global posture  | No discovered offensive meaning; screen at least English/French before launch and expand for approved countries. |
| Expansion fit   | Must stretch to Pro, community, creator, commerce, and professional offerings.                                   |
| App Store       | 2-30 characters and unique enough to satisfy Apple's metadata/copycat rules.                                     |
| Identifiers     | Lowercase ASCII root should work for domain, scheme, bundle suffix, App Group, email, and vendor projects.       |
| Legal posture   | No launch until counsel clears the exact word mark, use, classes, countries, and conditions in writing.          |

Apple limits the app name to 30 characters and tells developers to choose a
unique name and avoid third-party trademarks, misleading names, and copycats:
[App information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-information/)
and [App Review Guidelines 2.3.7, 4.1, and 5.2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

## Linguistic and voice criteria

- English launch pronunciation should not require accents or punctuation.
- French readability is a screening requirement for a Canadian launch; formal
  linguistic review remains required after launch countries are fixed.
- Avoid a pharmaceutical-sounding construction where possible because the app
  is health-adjacent but not a drug or medical device.
- Avoid mystical or ritual language if it weakens the evidence-aware position.
- A name may be abstract only if the subtitle immediately explains the product.

## Descriptor system

Working App Store presentation, subject to clearance and metadata review:

- App name: `[Cleared Brand]`
- Subtitle: `Skincare shelf & routines`
- Short promise: `Use what you own, with a calmer plan.`
- Paid tier: `[Cleared Brand] Pro`

Do not add `AI`, `scanner`, `dermatologist`, `safe`, or a competitor's mark to
the name or subtitle for search optimization.

## Scoring model

Names are scored out of 100 in [`BRAND-02-scored-longlist.md`](./BRAND-02-scored-longlist.md):

- distinctiveness: 20;
- category fit: 15;
- memorability: 15;
- pronunciation: 10;
- spelling: 10;
- expansion fit: 10;
- accessible-screen collision risk: 20, where a higher score means fewer
  observed risks on the surfaces actually checked.

A hard collision overrides the numerical score. Search results are screening
inputs, not legal conclusions.
